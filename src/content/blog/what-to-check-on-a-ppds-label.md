# What to check on a PPDS label

Natasha's Law applies to food that is packed on the premises before a customer selects it. The legal explanation sits on the PPDS page. This is the check before you print.

Read the food name. It should be the item you are actually packing.

Read the ingredient list. It should match the recipe in front of you, including compound ingredients broken down far enough for the allergens to show.

Read the allergens. The 14 regulated allergens that are present should be emphasised. If a supplier has changed, check that record before the next print.

Read the date. Your procedure decides whether that label carries a use-by or a best-before, and what the date is. InstaLabel prints the date from the settings you saved.

The 56×80 mm format has more room for a long ingredient list. The 60×40 mm format is the smaller label. Neither size removes the need to check the recipe.
